## Decisions Made

- **Criterion 3 is MET at six, and the number is stated as an outcome rather
  than defended.** The plan was written expecting five and phrases much of its
  guidance around that case. Amazon landed. Contorting the arithmetic to match
  the plan's prose would have been the same failure as contorting a criterion to
  match an outcome.
- **Criterion 1 stands UNMET and is not touched.** Target delisted the product;
  Dan declined the rewrite; the ROADMAP records the refusal. Five-of-six with one
  honest failure is worth more than six-of-six with one quiet edit.
- **`TARGET_RETAILER_COUNT` stays at 5.** Explicitly forbidden by the plan, and
  the reason survives the count going up: a threshold of 6 fires on the honest
  answer the next time Amazon walls.
- **The control lines were read out of `make verify` rather than re-measured.**
  `make verify` runs `scripts/control_check.py`; a standalone re-run is the same
  script producing the same lines at the cost of six more live requests, and
  Amazon throttles on cadence. Recorded as a substitution, not passed off as an
  independent run.
- **The service was restarted before the "service cycle" figure was taken.**
  Taking it beforehand would have published a pre-phase number under this
  phase's name.

## Issues Encountered

**A green `make verify` and a stale deployment are fully compatible, and only one
of them pages a person.** This is the second time this project has hit the shape:
Phase 2 shipped a verify that passed in a shell with `BOTY_BROWSER_PATH` exported
while the service could not find a browser. The README already warns about the
environment half of it. The *code* half — a service holding a Python process
loaded before the adapters existed — has no warning and no gate. `make verify`
cannot see it by construction, because it runs the tree rather than the daemon.

Nothing in this plan closes that permanently. What it does is establish the
practice: a phase that adds retailers restarts the service and reads the
service's own published cycle, and both figures go in the record.

**Two of the three `make verify` runs were mandated by the plan's own verify
blocks**, so the six live controls ran three times in ~13 minutes alongside three
`boty check` passes and two service cycles. Amazon saw roughly one request every
90 s or better throughout and refused nothing.
