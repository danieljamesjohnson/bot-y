<objective>
Attempt Amazon for real, then record what actually happened.

Phase 3 made **zero** requests to any Amazon product page. That is stated four
times in `docs/retailer-evidence.md` and it is true — so there is no technical
evidence about Amazon in either direction, and the rung-4 verdict rests entirely
on a reading of the Conditions of Use. Dan reversed that reading on 2026-08-03.
What is left is a question nobody has asked: **can we read the page at all?**

This plan asks it. Amazon is the retailer where the seller filter and the price
ceiling matter most — `amazon` is already in `MARKETPLACES`, its buy box is
frequently held by third parties, and a GO Plus + at $229 from a flipper is the
exact alert this project exists not to send.

**Both outcomes are legitimate, and only one of them is cheap.** A real technical
rung 4 — a wall, observed, at two rungs — is a genuine finding and a valid close
for this retailer. A rung 4 reached by not trying, or by calling a parser gap a
refusal, is not. This plan draws that line mechanically rather than trusting the
executor to hold it: a refusal requires anchored, rung-labelled observations in
the evidence log, `evidence_check` rule 4 already forces any successfully
captured page to become a configured watch, and rule 6 — added here — fails any
REFUSED verdict that cites no observation at all.

Purpose: settle Amazon on evidence from an actual attempt, and make "dropped for
a reason that is an observation, not a policy reading" (REQ-07a) a rule the tree
enforces rather than a sentence in a requirements document.
Output: a live Amazon observation, whichever branch it lands on, and a gate that
can tell the two apart.
</objective>
