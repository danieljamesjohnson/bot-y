<objective>
Attempt Amazon for real, then record what actually happened.

Phase 3 made **zero** requests to any Amazon product page. That is stated four
times in `docs/retailer-evidence.md` and it is true — so there is no technical
evidence about Amazon in either direction, and the rung-4 verdict rests entirely
on a reading of the Conditions of Use. Dan reversed that reading on 2026-08-03.
What is left is a question nobody has asked: **can we read the page at all?**

This plan asks it. Amazon is the retailer where the seller filter and the price
ceiling matter most — `amazon` is already in `MARKETPLACES`, its buy box is
frequently held by third parties, and a GO Plus + at $229 from a flipper is the
exact alert this project exists not to send.

**Both outcomes are legitimate, and only one of them is cheap.** A real technical
rung 4 — a wall, observed, at two rungs — is a genuine finding and a valid close
for this retailer. A rung 4 reached by not trying, or by calling a parser gap a
refusal, is not. This plan draws that line mechanically rather than trusting the
executor to hold it: the REFUSED branch requires three observations across two
rungs, rule 6 fails a refusal that cites fewer, `evidence_check` rule 4 already
forbids an unconfigured retailer whose page was captured, and the branch-A verify
requires a green Amazon control line rather than an exit code that merely is not
red.

Purpose: settle Amazon on evidence from an actual attempt, and make "dropped for
a reason that is an observation, not a policy reading" (REQ-07a) a rule the tree
enforces rather than a sentence in a requirements document.
Output: a live Amazon observation, whichever branch it lands on, and a gate that
can tell the two apart.
</objective>

REQ-07a's operative sentence is: *"A retailer is dropped only when it is
technically unreachable, and the reason recorded is the observation, not a policy
reading."* That is currently a sentence in `REQUIREMENTS.md` and nothing enforces
it. Phase 3 satisfied every gate in this tree while dropping two retailers on a
desk review, and no rule noticed. Make it a rule.

**1. Add the line form to `scripts/evidence_check.py`.**

Define an anchored regex beside `VERDICT_RE`, matching a whole line:

`^\*\*Refusal observed \(rung [1-3]\):\*\* .+$` with `re.MULTILINE`.

Anchored rather than a substring test, and write the reason in the comment: this
document states its own grammar in its preamble, which is exactly why
`VERDICT_RE` is anchored, and a substring rule here would be satisfied by the
paragraph that documents the rule. Add a `refusal_observations(body) -> list[str]`
helper alongside `verdict_lines` and `unprobed_lines`.

**2. Add rule 6 to `check_phase`,** appending to the same `problems` list. It has
a base bar and a higher bar, and the difference is the point:

- **Base bar, every roadmap retailer with exactly one section whose
  `verdict_lines` are `[REFUSED]`:** at least one refusal observation. Zero is a
  refusal nobody witnessed.
- **Higher bar, the `HARD_TWO` members (`target`, `amazon`) recorded REFUSED:**
  at least **two** observations, of which at least one names **rung 3**. This is
  the standard 03.1-02 Task 1 step 4 and 03.1-03 Task 1 step 4 both write their
  refusal branches to — two spaced rung-1 attempts plus one rung-3 attempt — and
  without it the branch is bounded by a promise rather than by the gate. A single
  `**Refusal observed (rung 1):** 503` would otherwise satisfy a rule whose
  branch demanded a walked ladder. Word the failure so it names the shortfall
  precisely: how many were found, and whether a rung-3 line was among them.

Word the base message so it names REQ-07a and says what the honest edits are —
record the observation that was actually made, or stop recording a refusal:

"rule 6 (a refusal must cite an observation): {evidence_path} records
`**Verdict: REFUSED**` for {display} and carries no
`**Refusal observed (rung N):** …` line. REQ-07a: a retailer is dropped only
when it is technically unreachable, and the reason recorded is the observation,
not a policy reading. Phase 3 dropped two retailers without making a single
product-page request and every gate in this tree stayed green. Either record what
came back when it was tried, or stop recording a refusal."

Note in the comment that rule 6 says nothing about REACHABLE or UNPROBED
sections — an UNPROBED retailer is *saying* nobody has looked and already carries
a dated, expiring claim, so demanding an observation from it would make the
honest state unrepresentable, which is the failure mode this file was written
against.

**3. Backfill Pokémon Center.** Its section already carries the observations the
higher bar would want — Imperva at HTTP 200 at rung 1, headless Chrome refused
twice at rung 3 — but as prose, not as anchored lines. Add them, quoting the
statuses and matched phrases the section already records. Add nothing new: this
is a re-statement of existing evidence in a machine-readable form, not a fresh
claim. Pokémon Center is not in `HARD_TWO`, so the base bar is what applies to

line.

**4. Update the evidence log's preamble** to document the new line, alongside the
three verdict forms it already spells out: what it looks like, that it is
required wherever a REFUSED verdict stands, that `HARD_TWO` retailers need two

`strip_fences` removes those before indexing, which is the mechanism that stops a
documented example being read as a record.

**5. Tests in `tests/test_evidence_check.py`,** using the synthetic-tree helpers
so nothing reads the real tree except where stated:

- a REFUSED section with no refusal-observation line → one rule-6 problem naming
  the retailer;
- the same section with one well-formed line → no problem **for a non-HARD_TWO
  retailer**, and still a problem for a `HARD_TWO` one. Both halves, so the
  higher bar is watched biting;
- a `HARD_TWO` REFUSED section with two rung-1 lines and no rung-3 line → a
