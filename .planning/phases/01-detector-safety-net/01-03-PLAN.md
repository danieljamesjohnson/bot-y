<objective>
Add type annotations across `boty/` and a committed mypy configuration.

Purpose: the extraction layer passes dicts and optionals between modules —
`Offer | None`, `float | None`, `list[Offer] | None` — where a None-handling
mistake produces a wrong stock verdict rather than a crash. That is exactly the
class of bug that is invisible in production. Types catch it at author time,
and with outside contributors coming in Phase 4 they catch it in review too.

Output: annotated modules and a `[tool.mypy]` section that makes the check
reproducible.
</objective>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 1: mypy configuration</name>
  <files>pyproject.toml</files>
  <read_first>pyproject.toml</read_first>
  <action>
Add a `[tool.mypy]` section to pyproject.toml targeting `python_version = "3.10"`, with `files = ["boty"]`, `warn_unused_ignores = true`, `warn_redundant_casts = true`, and `no_implicit_optional = true`.

Deliberately do NOT enable `strict = true`. The goal is catching real
None-handling errors in an 854-line codebase, not winning a strictness contest;
an over-strict config that contributors immediately start `# type: ignore`-ing
is worse than a moderate one they respect.

Add `ignore_missing_imports = true` for the `curl_cffi.*`, `apprise.*` and
`yaml.*` modules via `[[tool.mypy.overrides]]` — these ship without complete
stubs and their absence is not a signal about our code.

Ensure mypy is in the `dev` optional-dependency group (plan 01-02 may have
added it; if so leave it).
  </action>
  <verify>.venv/bin/python -c "import tomllib; d=tomllib.load(open('pyproject.toml','rb')); print(d['tool']['mypy'])"</verify>
  <acceptance_criteria>
