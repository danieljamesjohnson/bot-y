<objective>
06-01 closed REQ-17 by refusing to authorise an alert wherever a shipping cost could not be read.
It worked, it was measured, and it cost two of four product watches their ability to page. Dan was
shown that cost and chose a fourth option none of the three offered:

> *"I think where we don't know just send it. If the user gets there and it's 50 dollar shipping
> that's disappointing but it's worse to feel like you 'missed out'."*
> — Dan, 2026-08-11

This plan implements that decision and nothing else.

**What changes.** Where shipping cannot be established, the ceiling is applied to the **item price
alone** and the alert goes out. Nintendo and Amazon start alerting again.

**What does not change.** Where shipping IS resolvable the ceiling still measures the delivered
total — GameStop's $54.99 + $6.99 = $61.98 against its $80 ceiling — and a resolvable total ABOVE
the ceiling is still suppressed. **06-01's main win stays.**

**What mitigates the reopened hole.** The alert body stops being a sentence and becomes two
fields, in Dan's own rendering, the same shape either way:

    price: $54.99   shipping: unknown
    price: $54.99   shipping: $6.99

No "unverified" flag, no hedging sentence, and **no delivered total stated anywhere** — you cannot
add a number to `unknown`, so nothing is claimed. The unknown appears as a visible hole rather than
prose explaining one. That is this milestone's own thesis applied to the alert body: show each
field and its actual state.

**State plainly, and do not soften it: this reopens the specific hole REQ-17 was written to close.**
A $54.99 listing with $45 of hidden shipping will now page Dan. He accepted that trade explicitly,
his reasoning is recorded above, and the `shipping: unknown` field is what he is relying on to make
it survivable.

Purpose: a user reversed a decision. That is legitimate and it is precedented here — Phase 3.1
records exactly this when Dan reversed the Target/Amazon drop. It is **not** an agent rewording a
criterion so finished work looks successful, and the plan is built so the two cannot be confused.

Output: one predicate that decides whether a shipping figure is trusted, read by the verdict, the
`detail` and the alert body; one reversed branch in `alertable`; a restock body with a gate under
it for the first time in this project's life; five mutations watching all of it go red; and a
re-measured four-watch table so 06-06 records what landed rather than what was predicted.
</objective>

<decision>

## The rule, settled here

**Shipping resolvable → the ceiling measures the delivered total. Unchanged.**
**Shipping unresolvable → the ceiling measures the item price, and the alert goes out.**

Both halves are the reversal Dan asked for and neither is negotiable inside this plan. Two things
that look like the second half are **not** it, and each gets a mutation so a later reader cannot
drift into them:

- *"Where we don't know just send it"* does **not** mean send it at any price. A $229.99 reseller
  listing whose shipping nobody read is still refused: the item-price ceiling is the whole of what
  is left of that defence and it stays. **M28** rebuilds the misreading.
- *"Send it"* does **not** mean claim free shipping. An unread cost is `unknown` in the body and no
  delivered total is stated. **M17** rebuilds that claim.

## Where the honesty goes: the body, not a flag

`boty/notify.py` composes no diagnosis of its own and **must not start** — its own docstring says
so and `tests/test_alert_text.py` gates it. Rendering a field and its actual state is not a
diagnosis: it subtracts a claim rather than adding one, exactly as `_redact_store_numbers` does.

The vocabulary follows Phase 5's `CAUSE_UNKNOWN` precedent for a mechanical reason, not a stylistic
one: **one spelling of "nobody read this", in one constant, so a test can check it without matching
prose.** One formatter renders both fields, so price and shipping cannot drift into different
shapes.

**No total field, in either case.** A total that appears when shipping resolved and vanishes when it
did not is precisely the special-casing Dan's format avoids, and both addends are on the screen.

## What is NOT done here

- **`Availability` is not touched.** UNKNOWN is still never a verdict and never OUT_OF_STOCK. F1's
  two counts make it mechanical.
- **No `max_price` value changes, no watch is added, removed or re-scoped, no fixture is edited or
  re-captured, no new dependency is added, no `status.py` edit.**
- **`REQUIREMENTS.md` is not edited.** REQ-17's text stands as written. What happened is a user
  reversing a decision, recorded beside the original — Phase 3.1's format — never a criterion
  reworded to fit the code.
- **`ROADMAP.md` and `06-06-PLAN.md` are not edited.** See `<sequencing>`.
- No "v1", no "for now", nothing deferred. If something below cannot be built as written, stop and
  say so rather than shipping a narrower rule that reads like the wide one.

</decision>
