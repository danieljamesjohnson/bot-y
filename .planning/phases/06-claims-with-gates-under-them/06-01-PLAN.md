<objective>
The price ceiling currently measures `offer.price`. A $54.99 listing with $45 shipping walks
straight through it — one of only two defences against a reseller alert, defeated by a number the
page publishes and this project never reads. REQ-17 closes that: **the ceiling measures the
delivered total, and where the delivered total cannot be established it refuses to authorise an
alert rather than guessing.**

Purpose: this is criterion 1 of five in a phase whose whole subject is claims with gates under
them. The claim is `README.md:49` — *"an independent price ceiling as a second line of defence"* —
and today that claim is measurably narrower than it reads.

Output: `Offer.shipping` and `Result.shipping` carried the way `store` is carried, a
`Result.delivered_total` derived the way `degraded` is derived, one `alertable` guard replacing
one `alertable` guard, three mutations watching all of it go red, and a **measured** table of
which GO Plus + watches can still alert — which is a real user-visible cost this plan produces
rather than absorbs.
</objective>

verdict can change under any edit in this plan.** That is the measured blast radius: this work
cannot break `make verify`'s control stage.

Three consequences this plan honours:

1. The two-signal rule is still the right rule — it just resolves **one** fixture, not two.
   `goplusplus` → shipping `0.0`; `milk-control` → **unresolved**. That is the correct and
   fail-safe answer, and it costs nothing: milk is a control and carries no ceiling (F1).
2. **`06-PATTERNS.md`'s "every numeric shipping field null" is not quite true either.** There IS a
   non-null number on `milk-control`: `7.95`, and it is a **from-store DELIVERY fee on a

   shipping cost from a real field — the same class of error as F3, arrived at from a different
   direction. **`fulfillmentPrice` is not read anywhere in this plan.**
3. `fulfillmentPrice` is `None` on one fixture and a `dict` on the other — **the same key, two
   types**, exactly like F3. Anything that ever reads it must type-check first. Nothing here does.

Also measured and deliberately not used: `priceInfo.additionalFees.estimatedTotalPrice` exists on
both fixtures and equals the item price on both (`229.99`, `2.42`). It has **never been observed
differing from the item price**, so adopting it as "the delivered total" would be adopting an
untested claim. It is a candidate for a later plan with a capture behind it, not evidence today.

### F3 — Nintendo's prose, and the wrong verdict a lenient reader produces

Nintendo's sentence says free shipping over $50. The item is $54.99. **The true shipping cost is
zero.** A regex over that sentence pulls out `$6.99` and yields a delivered total of **$61.98 for
an item that ships free** — an inflated total that can suppress a genuine MSRP restock at the one
retailer that makes the product and lists it at MSRP with no marketplace attached
(`README.md` Nintendo row). That is a wrong *verdict* produced by a careless fix, in a phase about
claims with gates under them, and it is the same class as 05-01's rejected `"0"` sentinel: reading
presentation text as a fact. **No shipping figure is parsed out of prose anywhere in this plan.**

### F4 — Float arithmetic. `54.99 + 6.99` is not `61.98`

Measured: `54.99 + 6.99 == 61.980000000000004`, so `54.99 + 6.99 <= 61.98` is **False**, while
`<= 80` is True and `<= 60` is False. Tests use `pytest.approx` for the sum and ceilings of `80`
and `60`, which are unambiguous. **The delivered total is never rounded** — rounding would invent
precision no retailer stated, and it would move a boundary case in the permissive direction.

### F5 — `status.json` gains nothing, and that is mechanical rather than a promise

`boty/status.py` builds its watch rows field-by-field (`"price": r.price` at `:95`); it does not
`asdict` a `Result`. So adding a field to `Result` publishes nothing new, and **no `status.py`
edit is in this plan**. Criterion 1 does not ask for a shipping key the way Phase 5's criterion 1
asked for `store`, and no source artifact asks for one. Recorded so its absence is not read as an
omission.

### F6 — M4's anchor, and the trap in the obvious re-point

M4's `search` is verbatim the three lines
`if self.price is None:` / `return False` / `return self.price <= self.watch.max_price`.
Any delivered-total edit moves the last of them, `apply_mutation` raises `HarnessError`
(*"The source drifted away from this mutation"*), and `make verify` dies rather than quietly
dropping a mutation. Re-pointing it in the same commit is a required edit on Phase 4's M2
precedent, not a surprise.

**The trap:** if `alertable` keeps a `price is None` guard *ahead of* a delivered-total guard,
then flipping the price guard's `return False` to `return True` changes nothing — the total is
also `None`, the next guard returns `False`, the mutation SURVIVES, and `make verify` fails
reporting an uncaught M4. Task 2's structure removes the redundant guard for exactly this reason,
and M4 re-points onto the one guard that remains.

<decision>

## The rule, settled here and argued in the code

Three options were measured in `06-PLAN-OUTLINE.md` § *Finding 1*. **Option 1 (strict) is what
lands, alone.**

- **Option 2 — fall back to the item price when a retailer publishes no shipping at all — is
  OUT.** It reopens the exact hole REQ-17 closes. Walmart's paid-marketplace payload is
  *unobserved* (F2), so "publishes nothing" and "publishes something we did not read" are
  indistinguishable on the retailer this criterion exists for.
- **Option 3 — a per-watch operator declaration in Phase 5's `store_id` shape — is not taken in
  this plan**, and the reason is scope rather than merit: it needs `boty/config.py`, which
  `06-PLAN-OUTLINE.md` § *Multi-source coverage audit* lists as out of scope for every plan in
  this phase. It may be added on top of option 1 by a later plan; it may never replace it.
- **Option 1 is the floor,** and the tiebreaker is already recorded rather than re-derived:
  `REQUIREMENTS.md` § Non-Functional — *"**Trustworthiness over coverage.** Where they conflict,
  correctness wins. … This is the tiebreaker for every scoping decision."*

**Where the UNKNOWN goes: `alertable`, not `Availability`.** `tests/test_models.py:80-92` already
decided this shape — *"A ceiling that cannot be evaluated must not authorise an alert."* An
unestablished delivered total is a ceiling that cannot be evaluated. So `alertable` returns
`False`, and **`Availability` is not touched by anything in this plan.** Driving availability to
UNKNOWN would make the page's own stock statement disappear because of a *pricing* question, and
would put a permanent UNKNOWN on Nintendo and Amazon with no path back. The standing rule
*"UNKNOWN is never a verdict, and never OUT_OF_STOCK"* is a rule against **resolving** an unknown
into a verdict; `alertable == False` resolves nothing and moves in the fail-safe direction.

## What this costs, stated before it is built

Measured from F1, at today's `max_price: 80`:

| GO Plus + watch | Delivered total establishable? | Can still alert on a restock? |
|---|---|---|
| GameStop | **Yes** — 54.99 + 6.99 = 61.98, under 80 | **Yes** |
| Walmart | **Yes** — 229.99 + 0.00, over 80 (and the seller filter suppresses it first) | Yes, when a first-party offer under the ceiling appears |
| Nintendo | **No** — `shippingDetails` is prose (F3) | **NO — this watch stops being alertable** |
| Amazon | **No** — the reader is a button (F1) | **NO — this watch stops being alertable** |

Two of four product watches stop being able to page Dan, and one of them is the watch most likely
to be a genuine MSRP restock. **This plan does not absorb that.** It is not softened, the ceilings
are NOT raised to compensate, and no watch is edited to work around it. Task 3 records the table
above as a *re-measurement against the built tree*, and `06-06` carries it to Dan at a blocking
checkpoint. This milestone exists so consequences are stated rather than absorbed.

No "v1", no "static for now", no "resolve it properly in a later phase". Nothing here is deferred
or thinned. If something below cannot be built as written, stop and say so rather than shipping a
narrower rule that reads like the wide one.
