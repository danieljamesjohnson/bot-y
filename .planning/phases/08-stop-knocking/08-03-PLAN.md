## The number this plan settles, stated once and up front

`MAX_BACKOFF_SECONDS` is `6 * 60 * 60` = **21600**, unchanged.
`COOLOFF_SECONDS` is `3 * 24 * 60 * 60` = **259200**, fixed by `08-02` and not re-decided here.

`LONGEST_WAIT_SECONDS = max(MAX_BACKOFF_SECONDS, COOLOFF_SECONDS)` = **259200 seconds, three days.**
`STATE_MAX_AGE_SECONDS = LONGEST_WAIT_SECONDS` = **259200 seconds, three days**, up from 21600.

**It is written as the expression and not as its current winner, and that is the whole of the
design.** `STATE_MAX_AGE_SECONDS = COOLOFF_SECONDS` would be shorter, would produce the identical
number today, and would be wrong tomorrow: it says *the window is the cool-off*, when what the
constant's own comment argues is that the window is **one full longest-wait-length window**. If a
later phase raised `MAX_BACKOFF_SECONDS` above `COOLOFF_SECONDS`, or shortened the cool-off, the
short form would silently understate the longest wait — and a window shorter than a wait the module
actually produces is precisely the defect this plan exists to remove, rebuilt from the other end
by the fix for it.

## The six criteria, and which one is this plan's

  1. After a bounded number of consecutive refusals, the wait returned for that retailer is measured in **days**, asserted against literal expected seconds — not against "greater than the old ceiling"
  2. A retailer in cool-off is requested **exactly once** when the cool-off expires, not once per cycle. The single-probe behaviour is asserted over a simulated sequence of cycles, not inferred from the interval
  3. The total number of requests made to a retailer that **never** recovers, over a simulated 30 days, is a **stated number** and is strictly smaller than the same simulation under the current rule. Both numbers are recorded
  4. A retailer that answers during its probe returns to its normal cadence **immediately**, and the cool-off state is cleared — the failure mode where a recovered retailer stays throttled is asserted against
  5. The cool-off **survives a restart**, the same guarantee the existing backoff already carries, and is discarded when stale by the existing rule rather than a second one
  6. `make verify-offline` exits 0, with at least one new mutation registered, observed CAUGHT, and anchored on **behaviour** rather than on message text

**This plan owns criterion 5 and nothing else.** Criteria 1, 2, 3 and 4 are `08-01`'s and `08-02`'s
and are re-run here as regression only. **Criterion 6 is `08-04`'s**: `make verify-offline` is run
in Task 3 to prove the tree is green with this change in it, no new mutation ident is registered
here, and M42 is still free when this plan ends. Task 3 says that in its own words rather than

## The gap `08-02` wrote down, and what closing it costs

`08-02` § *The recorded one-wave gap* states it exactly, and this plan closes it in the same terms:
between wave 2 and wave 3 the tree holds a **three-day cool-off that a restart discards after six
hours**, because `Pacer.load`'s two-sided window throws away any refusal record stamped more than
`MAX_BACKOFF_SECONDS` ago — which, once a retailer is in a three-day cool-off, is every record it
will ever have. Nothing was deployed mid-phase, no plan in this phase restarts the daemon, and a
restart is Dan's call, so it was a recorded gap and not a shipped defect. **After Task 1 it is
closed, and the SUMMARY must say so in those words rather than leaving a reader to infer it.**

Closing it has a price and the price is stated rather than absorbed. Widening the window widens
**both** things it governs:

1. **The refusal counts** survive up to three days instead of six hours. That is the point.
2. **The paging memory (`warned`) survives up to three days instead of six hours** — and that is a
   real behavioural change to a different subsystem, arriving as a side effect of a shared
   constant. It is argued in Task 2 § *Why the shared constant is required rather than merely
   tidy*, and it carries this plan's third prohibition.

## The residual, measured rather than waved at

The window now **equals** the longest wait, so a record can age out only in the sliver between the
moment a cool-off expires and the moment the next cycle actually probes. `record` re-stamps
`refused_at` at every refusal, so in a steady cool-off the stamp's age runs from 0 to 259200 and
resets; the schedule advances on `cli.watch_loop`'s synthetic clock with its own jitter, so the
probe lands **at or slightly after** the wait rather than exactly on it. A restart inside that
sliver discards the record, and the retailer resumes at its standing interval and climbs the
backoff again from the bottom.

**Projected size of the sliver: one cycle, roughly 300 s out of 259200 — about 0.12% of the
window.** It points the same direction the withdrawn six-hour window pointed 100% of the time, so
the change is a strict improvement rather than a trade. The option not taken is deliberate: adding
slack (a window of, say, `COOLOFF_SECONDS * 2`) would remove the sliver and would be **re-choosing
a number the constant's own comment says must be derived** — buying 0.12% with the one property
that keeps the two constants from drifting apart. Recorded, not fixed. If the residual ever bites,
that is the change to consider, and it should be considered as a change to
`LONGEST_WAIT_SECONDS`' definition rather than as a second constant.

## The case above the window, and why it costs nothing

`current_interval` returns `max(st.interval, ...)`, and `st.interval` is operator-configured with a
floor and **no upper bound** (`config._interval`). So `current_interval` can return more than
`LONGEST_WAIT_SECONDS` — at a standing interval of, say, a week. Does the window then fail to cover
the module's waits?

**No, and it is provable rather than arguable.** Whenever `st.interval >= LONGEST_WAIT_SECONDS`,
the outer `max` returns `st.interval` at *every* refusal depth: at 0 refusals by the
`not st.refusals` branch, from 1 to 29 because the capped backoff cannot exceed 21600, and at 30
and beyond because the cool-off cannot exceed 259200. The depth is therefore **not load-bearing at
all** at such an interval, so a depth aged out there changes nothing about when the retailer is
asked. `LONGEST_WAIT_SECONDS` is the longest wait the module's own **policy** produces; above it
the wait is the operator's standing decision, which no persisted count influences. Task 1 asserts
this rather than leaving it as a paragraph.

## Criterion 5's second clause: there is not a third rule, and there is barely a third use

The existing rule is **one expression applied at exactly two sites** in `Pacer.load`: the refusal
counts (`boty/pacing.py:542`) and the paging memory (`boty/pacing.py:576`), both spelled
`if not 0.0 <= now - <stamp> <= STATE_MAX_AGE_SECONDS: continue`.

The outline calls a cool-off *"its third use, not a second rule."* Reading the code, **it is less
than that — it is not a new use at all.** The cool-off is carried entirely by `refusals`, which
site one already governs, with the same stamp field, the same guard and the same constant. This
plan adds no site, no comparison and no constant beside it; it changes one right-hand side. That
is a stronger statement than the outline's and it is the one the source supports, so it is the one
made here. Task 1 proves it three ways rather than asserting it: a source count of the guard sites
that stays at two, a future-dated stamp still discarded at cool-off depth, and one `load` restoring
both halves together.
