## The literals, fixed here so 08-03 and 08-04 do not have to guess

`REFUSALS_BEFORE_COOLOFF = 30`

- The phase goal says *"30-odd times"*, and 30 is the number that makes collision A **bind**: the
  existing `test_the_backoff_is_capped_so_a_monitor_does_not_quietly_stop_monitoring`
  (`tests/test_pacing.py:176`) drives exactly `range(30)` and asserts the old ceiling. Choosing 31
  or more would let that test survive untouched, which is cheaper and worse — the sentence REQ-22
  exists to overrule would still be standing, unremarked, at the exact boundary criterion 1 is
  about.
- It sits comfortably under `MAX_PERSISTED_REFUSALS = 64`, so a count restored from disk can
  actually cross it. A threshold at or above the clamp would be a threshold that can never be
  reached from a restart.
- **The outline's third constraint is now vacuous and that is recorded rather than quietly
  satisfied.** The outline asks for a threshold above `cli.REFUSALS_BEFORE_PAGING = 5`. That
  constant **no longer exists**: `boty/cli.py:432-451` records that it and
  `_refusal_is_entrenched` were deleted on 2026-08-12 under Dan's no-paging rule, and
  `test_the_clamp_never_restores_a_shallower_wait_than_the_cap`
  (`tests/test_pacing.py:884`) was re-anchored that day for the same reason. 30 is above 5 anyway,
  so the constraint is satisfied — but it is satisfied against a deleted symbol, and the honest
  form of that is to say so. (`boty/pacing.py:175` still names `cli.REFUSALS_BEFORE_PAGING` in the
  `MAX_PERSISTED_REFUSALS` comment. That is a pre-existing stale cross-reference, it is **not**
  this plan's to fix, and no task below touches it.)

`COOLOFF_SECONDS = 3 * 24 * 60 * 60` — that is **259200.0** seconds.

- REQ-22 says *"a period measured in days"*. Three days is unambiguously days-plural; two is the
  smallest thing that can be called days and reads as a rounding-up of "a couple".
- Written as `3 * 24 * 60 * 60` in the source, on `MAX_BACKOFF_SECONDS`' own precedent
  (`6 * 60 * 60`), so the units are legible at the definition site. It is a **literal**: no
  exponentiation, no `BACKOFF_FACTOR`, no derivation from the cap. That is what makes the
  precision probe in `must_haves.truths` true rather than hoped for.
- Three days rather than seven because `COOLOFF_SECONDS` is what `08-03` will derive
  `STATE_MAX_AGE_SECONDS` from. A week-long staleness window would hold a refusal record as
  evidence for a week, which weakens at length the stale-file answer the module docstring gives.
  **That staleness-window argument is the WHOLE of the duration decision, and it stands alone.**
  The request-count column in the table below is a projection by the plan writer, it is labelled as
  one, and it is therefore **illustrative and not a decision input** — it is printed so a reader can
  see the shape of the curve (the returns flatten fast past two days), not so the choice can be
  read as bought with it. A differing measurement of that column does **not** reopen three days:
  the only measurement that could is one showing a three-day staleness window is itself too long to
  believe a refusal record for, and that is `08-03`'s to take. Saying this explicitly is the point —
  a paragraph that disclaims its own numbers and then leans on them has disclaimed nothing.

## The numbers, and how they were reached

Projected 2026-08-28 by the plan writer, by replicating the scheduling arithmetic
(`now + interval * 0.5 >= due_at`, then `max(interval, min(interval * 2.0 ** refusals, 21600))`)
in a scratch script over 8640 cycles of 300 s — 30 days — against a retailer that never recovers
and never restarts:

| Rule | 30-day request count |
|---|---|
| current (fixed 6 h ceiling, applied indefinitely) | **125** |
| threshold 30, cool-off 2 days | 41 |
| **threshold 30, cool-off 3 days (chosen)** | **37** |
| threshold 30, cool-off 5 days | 34 |
| threshold 30, cool-off 7 days | 33 |

**The rows other than the chosen one are ILLUSTRATIVE.** They show the curve flattening; they are
not why three days was chosen, and § *The literals* above says so in its own words. The duration
rests on the staleness-window argument alone.

**These are a projection, not a measurement, and the distinction is load-bearing.** They were
computed against a re-implementation of the arithmetic, not against the real `Pacer`. The
executor must run the real simulation against the real class and record what it actually returns.
**If the measured number differs from 37, the measured number wins**, it goes into the test as the
literal, and the difference is recorded in the SUMMARY with the reason. Rewriting 37 into the test
without running it is the one unforgivable move wearing a measurement's clothes.

`08-01` owns the before-number independently. If `08-01` recorded something other than 125, **use
`08-01`'s number** — it was measured against the unmodified tree, which this plan can no longer
do — and record the discrepancy rather than reconciling it silently.

keeps in exactly one place on purpose (`boty/pacing.py:380-415`). Two copies of a rule are two
things to edit and they only have to disagree once. The anchor drift is the price, it is a price
this registry has paid five times with a written protocol for paying it, and paying it is cheaper
than a second copy of the module's central invariant.

## The recorded one-wave gap (state this in the SUMMARY, do not close it here)

Between this plan and `08-03` the tree holds a **three-day cool-off that a restart discards after
six hours.** `STATE_MAX_AGE_SECONDS` is still `MAX_BACKOFF_SECONDS` (`boty/pacing.py:151`), so
`Pacer.load`'s two-sided window throws away any refusal record stamped more than 6 h ago — which,
once a retailer is in a three-day cool-off, is every record it will ever have.

This is deliberate and it is the outline's decision, not an oversight. Collision 1's
`STATE_MAX_AGE_SECONDS` re-derivation lands in `08-03` **so that `08-03`'s tests are genuinely red
before its one-line fix rather than red against a synthetic revert.** Nothing is deployed
mid-phase, no `systemctl restart boty` is a deliverable of any plan in this phase, and a restart
is Dan's call. It is a recorded gap, not a shipped defect, and the difference is that it is
written down here before it exists.

A second thing is handed to `08-03` rather than decided here: after this plan `refusals` past 30
means something it did not mean before, which is exactly the kind of policy-units change
`STATE_VERSION`'s comment (`boty/pacing.py:96-140`) says a bump is owed for. The outline assigns
that argument — and the argument for **no** new `_RetailerState` field — to `08-03`. This plan
adds no persisted structure, so it does not pre-empt it, and it names the question rather than
leaving `08-03` to find it.
