## The six criteria this phase is judged on (verbatim from ROADMAP.md, unedited)

  1. After a bounded number of consecutive refusals, the wait returned for that retailer is measured in **days**, asserted against literal expected seconds — not against "greater than the old ceiling"
  2. A retailer in cool-off is requested **exactly once** when the cool-off expires, not once per cycle. The single-probe behaviour is asserted over a simulated sequence of cycles, not inferred from the interval
  3. The total number of requests made to a retailer that **never** recovers, over a simulated 30 days, is a **stated number** and is strictly smaller than the same simulation under the current rule. Both numbers are recorded
  4. A retailer that answers during its probe returns to its normal cadence **immediately**, and the cool-off state is cleared — the failure mode where a recovered retailer stays throttled is asserted against
  5. The cool-off **survives a restart**, the same guarantee the existing backoff already carries, and is discarded when stale by the existing rule rather than a second one
  6. `make verify-offline` exits 0, with at least one new mutation registered, observed CAUGHT, and anchored on **behaviour** rather than on message text

## M42, argued before it is written

### What it anchors on

**The cool-off branch inside `Pacer.current_interval`** — specifically the whole indented source line
carrying the comparison against `REFUSALS_BEFORE_COOLOFF` in the conditional expression `08-02` put
inside the existing `max(st.interval, ...)`. Removing it changes **when a request is made**: a
retailer past the threshold goes back to being asked every six hours instead of every three days.

Not the constants. `COOLOFF_SECONDS`' value is already pinned by `08-02`'s hand-written
literal-seconds table at 29, 30 and 31 refusals at two standing intervals, so a mutation on the value
would be caught by a gate that already exists and would defend less — `08-PATTERNS.md` § conventions,
rule 5, says this in as many words. Not `skipped_reason`'s prose either: the registry's own rule 3 is
that a mutation anchors on observable behaviour and never on message text, and criterion 6 repeats it.

### What it defends that nothing else does

Two claims, both checkable against the file rather than asserted, and both **verified in Task 1
before the ident is kept**:

**1. It is the only ident that removes the cool-off and nothing else.** After `08-02` re-pointed them,
`M33` and `M38` both anchor on the same `return max(...)` block. M33 replaces the whole block with
`return st.interval`; M38 replaces it with a bare `min(...)` that strips the outer `max`. Each of them
therefore takes the cool-off down **as collateral** while breaking its own rule at the same time, so
neither can distinguish *"the cool-off died"* from *"the accessor died"*. A kill set that is a superset
of two others still tells a reader something neither of them can: **which rule died.** That is the
difference between an ident that raises the denominator and one that localises a failure.

**2. Its consequence is a divergence between what the monitor SAYS and what it DOES.** M42 leaves
`skipped_reason`'s cool-off arm entirely intact — that arm is guarded on the same threshold but by its
own independent comparison, one method along, which M42's anchor does not touch. So under M42 a
retailer past the threshold is still *described* as cooling off on the status page and in the daemon
log, while `record` has quietly gone back to scheduling it six hours out. A surface that says one
thing while the monitor does another is this project's core defect stated one level up, and no
existing ident in this registry covers a divergence between those two methods.

**If Task 1's measurement contradicts either claim, record that and re-argue the ident.** In
particular, if `08-02` chose an M38 `replace` that PRESERVES the cool-off, claim 1 is weaker than
written and the honest form is to say so in M42's comment block rather than to leave the stronger
sentence standing.

### The cost it carries, stated rather than buried

M42's anchor sits **inside** the block M33 and M38 anchor on, so one edit to `current_interval`'s
return drifts **three** anchors instead of two. That is a real maintenance cost and it is accepted for
a stated reason: `apply_mutation` raises `HarnessError` when its anchor is not found and `main` turns
that into exit 2 with *"This is not a result. Nothing was proved about the test suite either way."*
Drift is therefore a **stop**, never a silent reduction — the harness's own error message says
skipping silently *"would quietly reduce the check to two mutations while still printing a total"*,
which is the failure mode being avoided. Three loud anchors are cheaper than one quiet one.

The alternative — anchoring M42 somewhere outside that block — was considered and rejected: there is
nowhere else the cool-off decides when a request is made. `record` computes its wait THROUGH the
accessor, which is the whole reason `08-02` put the branch inside it.

## Collision B, and why it is decided here rather than discovered

`served/boty/index.html:118` is a three-band duration formatter, and `08-PLAN-OUTLINE.md` § B, `08-01`
§ Collision B and `08-02` § Collision B all defer the presentation choice to this plan **and all three
say the deliverable is the decision, not the change.** `72h` is an acceptable outcome. So is a day
band. What is not acceptable is the number arriving on the page without anybody having decided.

**The plan writer measured it on 2026-08-28** with a real JavaScript runtime, evaluating the page's own
arrow function and a hypothetical four-band variant side by side:

| input | today | with an 86400 day band |
|---|---|---|
| 259200 (a three-day cool-off) | `72h` | `3d` |
| 262800 (a reading one hour past that cool-off) | `73h` | `3d` |
| 21600 (the six-hour cap) | `6h` | `6h` |
| `NaN` (the measured silent-failure input) | `0h` | `0d` |

**The recommendation this plan carries, and Task 2 re-measures before adopting: keep the three bands
and change nothing.** Four reasons, each a fact about the tree rather than a preference:

1. **The single site collapses.** `fmtDur` renders a *cadence* in exactly one place — `ageTag`'s warn
   branch, `${fmtAge(age)} > ${fmtDur(interval)}`, whose stated job is *"both numbers are shown so you
   can see which threshold the verdict was taken against"*. Today that reads `73h ago > 72h`. With a
   day band it reads `3d ago > 3d` — a true `>` rendered as an apparent equality, in the one tag that
   exists to show a comparison. That is a legibility regression wearing the shape of an improvement.
2. **It falsifies a recorded byte-identical claim.** The comment above `fmtDur` records that
   *"`fmtAge`'s output is UNCHANGED for every input — same bands, same coercions, same suffix"*. A
   fourth band changes it for every input at or above the boundary, so the claim would owe a dated
   reversal in the house form on a page whose comment gates have gone red on prose twice already.
3. **It falsifies a measured silent-failure analysis.** `renderStatus`'s comment records, measured on
   2026-08-20, that a missing `updated` key yields `NaN`, that `NaN` *"is less than neither of
   `fmtDur`'s two band boundaries"*, and that it therefore renders `0h`. A third boundary makes that
   sentence false in both its count and its outcome.
4. **It would have to be paid for twice, with no gate to notice if it were paid once.**
   `boty/cli.py:122-145`'s `_age` is a deliberate hand-written mirror of these bands — its own
   docstring says *"THE BANDS ARE DELIBERATELY `served/boty/index.html`'s. A reader comparing `boty
   check` to the page must read the same number rather than translating between two vocabularies."*
   And **nothing in the suite pins the two together**: `grep -rn "5400" tests/` finds it only in
   `tests/test_dashboard.py`, where the assertion counts occurrences in the HTML. Moving one and not
   the other is a silent divergence between two surfaces this repository went to trouble to keep
   identical.

**The counter-argument, recorded rather than omitted.** `72h` is a number a reader has to divide to
understand as "three days", and a monitor that makes its reader do arithmetic is worse than one that
does not. That cost is real and it is accepted, for a bounded reason: the cadence renders on the page
only in the branch where a reading is ALREADY older than its cadence, which for a cool-off retailer
means a reading already more than three days old — a row that is by then carrying a warn tag whose
whole point is that something is wrong. The number a reader meets in the ordinary case is the *age*,
not the cadence, and the age of a healthy row is never days-scale.
