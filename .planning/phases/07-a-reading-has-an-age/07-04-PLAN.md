<objective>
Make every configured watch have a row, and make a remembered row say that it is remembered.

`status.write` rebuilds `watches` from `results` every cycle, and `run_once` filters `watches` down
to those the pacer says are due. So a paced-out watch does not leave a stale row behind — **it has no
row at all.** This plan emits one row per configured watch: rows for watches read this cycle are
unchanged plus `checked: true`, and the rest carry the availability the ledger remembers, the stamp
the ledger holds for it, and `checked: false`.

**Purpose.** REQ-21 says a row read four seconds ago and one last read two days ago are
byte-identical in shape and the page presents both as current. Measured, the page presents the old
one **not at all** — which is not better, and is arguably worse: *"not watched this cycle"* and
*"watched, out of stock"* are indistinguishable when one of them is simply missing, and a row that
is absent cannot answer Dan's question (*"so they are out of stock as of when?"*) any more than an
undated one can. It also leaves criterion 3 with almost nothing to bind on: every row in a running
daemon's file is at most one cycle old by construction, so the `status.json` half of that criterion
would bind only on a stopped daemon, and its `boty check` half — a surface that re-reads every watch
— could not bind at all. **Carrying the remembered reading onto the surfaces is required by criteria
3 and 4, not added to them.**

**Output.** A second comprehension in `status.write`, in the shape the `retailers` array beside it
already uses; `cli._remembered`, the one reader that pairs an availability with its age; two new
mutations, each watched going red by hand before the harness ever ran either; and one test that
states the whole plan in a sentence — **13 rows published while N were fetched.**

## The five criteria this phase is judged on (verbatim from ROADMAP.md, unedited)

  1. Every `Result` records when it was read, and that time is published per watch in `status.json`
  2. A reading with no recorded time is shown as UNKNOWN age, never as current — watched going red
  3. A reading older than its retailer's current interval is presented as stale in `status.json`, `boty check` and the dashboard, and the staleness is derived from the retailer's own pacing rather than a fixed clock
  4. The age survives a service restart, so a restart cannot make a two-day-old reading look fresh
  5. `make verify-offline` exits 0, and every gate this phase adds has been watched going red

**This plan owns the ROW half of criterion 3** — the thing that has to exist before staleness can be
presented at all. The threshold is 07-03's; the three renderings are 07-05's. It also carries a
piece of criterion 2: a watch nobody has ever resolved publishes `unknown` with no age, never `now`.
Criterion 5 binds here as the red-watch on **M34** and **M35**.

## THE THREE-WAY PARTITION OF A ROW, WHICH IS THE WHOLE DESIGN

Every key belongs to exactly one class, and the class decides what a remembered row carries:

| Class | Keys | On a remembered row |
|---|---|---|
| **Config facts** — true whether or not anybody looked | `name`, `retailer`, `url`, `control`, `store_pinned` | published, from the `Watch` |
| **The remembered reading** — what the ledger holds | `availability`, `read_at` | published, from the ledger; `unknown` / `null` where it holds nothing |
| **Facts about the act of reading** — nobody performed one | `price`, `detail`, `rung`, `extraction`, `degraded`, `store` | **`null`**, never a default |
| **Authority** — belongs to a reading somebody took | `alertable` | **`false`, stated** |
| **Provenance** — the new key | `checked` | `false` |

`null` and not a default is the `store`-as-`0` rule for the third time in this file
(`status.py:53-57`, `status.py:136-141`). Applied here it is sharper than an analogy: `detail: ""`
is a value the fresh path already produces and means *"the page said nothing worth repeating"*;
`null` never appears on a fresh row, so it is **distinguishable**, which is the entire reason to
prefer it. `degraded: false` on a memory would be a confidence claim about a reading nobody took —
so `rung`, `extraction` and their derived flag go out as `null` together.

**Fresh and remembered rows carry the same keys in the same order.** That is a decision, not an
accident: it means `checked` and `read_at` are the *only* things that distinguish them, which is
REQ-21's sentence — *"byte-identical in shape"* — converted from a defect into a stated property
with exactly two fields carrying the difference.

## `alertable` IS STATED, AND THE CLAIM ABOUT IT IS MADE CAREFULLY

`alertable` is `False` on a remembered row, written as a literal rather than derived. An alert
decision belongs to a reading somebody took, and a memory has no standing to make one.

**State exactly what that does and does not prevent, because overclaiming here would be this
phase's own defect.** `status.json`'s `alertable` does **not** send anything: pushes come from
`run_once`'s `alerts` list, built from `Result` objects, and no remembered row ever becomes a
`Result`. What `alertable: true` on a remembered row would be is a **published claim, on a served
page, that a reading nobody took is worth waking somebody for** — rendered by the dashboard, and
available to every future consumer of this file, of which this project already has three.

And it is not hypothetical arithmetic. Measured against today's ledger: **8 of the 13 remembered
entries are `in_stock`**; 6 of those are controls, and the remaining **two are the GameStop
`TRANSITION —` watches**, which `config/products.yaml:330` records as deliberately not controls.
Those two watches have no `max_price`, so `alertable` derived from a remembered `in_stock` would be
`True`. **So a naive inheritance publishes two false buy-signals on every cycle GameStop is paced
out** — and GameStop is on a 900-second override, so that is most of them.

## SCOPE FENCE

- **Nothing here changes what is fetched.** The pacer's decisions, `run_once`'s due-filter, the
  backoff, the cap and the politeness cadence are untouched. This plan changes what is *reported*.
  That is a claim with a test behind it (Task 2), which counts checker calls against published rows
  in one assertion.
- **Nothing here computes staleness.** No stamp is compared to anything, no `stale` flag is derived,
  no tag is rendered, no threshold is read. `intervals`, the keyword 07-03 added, is passed through
  untouched — **the interval accessor is 07-03's and this plan neither calls it nor re-derives it.**
- **Nothing here renders.** The wording of a tag, its `title=` sentence and `cli._age_tag` are
  **07-05's**. This plan makes the row exist and makes it carry its provenance.
- `boty/models.py`, `boty/retailers.py`, `boty/monitor.py`, `boty/pacing.py` and
  `served/boty/index.html` are **not touched**.
- No clock is frozen, injected or monkeypatched. Aged ledgers are constructed by subtraction from
  the real clock — `tests/test_pacing.py:585-591`'s method, as every plan in this phase has used.
