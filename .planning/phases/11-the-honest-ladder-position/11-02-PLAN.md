## The finding that produced this plan, and it was not planned for

`11-01` set out to write a dated verdict into `docs/retailer-evidence.md` for each of Amazon,
Target and Walmart. **Two of the three had a section to write it in. Walmart did not.**

Measured 2026-09-14, `grep -n "^## " docs/retailer-evidence.md` → 16 headings. Five are retailers
(Amazon, Best Buy, Nintendo, Pokémon Center, Target) and eleven are cross-cutting or phase records.
**There is no `## Walmart` section and no `## GameStop` section** — and both retailers are
configured, shipped, control-verified and advertised as ✅ Working in `README.md`.

**`scripts/evidence_check.py --phase` passes anyway, and it is not a bug in the run — it is the
rule.** Rule 2 opens with:

```python
for retailer, display in ROADMAP_RETAILERS.items():
    if retailer in configured:
        continue
```

So the *entire* per-retailer evidence obligation applies only to retailers that are **not**
configured. A retailer that ships is exempt from having to say what was measured about it.

### CORRECTED BEFORE EXECUTION — this was not an oversight, and the first draft of this plan said it was

The paragraph above is kept and the correction placed under it, because writing a plan around
"nobody noticed this" when the tree says otherwise is exactly the failure this phase is about.

**It was noticed, argued and deliberately allowed.** Rule 5's own comment block, case 1, names both
retailers:

> *NO SECTION AT ALL. GameStop and Walmart are configured and have never had a section in the
> evidence log; rule 2 requires one only from retailers that are NOT configured. Demanding one here
> would redden the shipped tree over a gap that is not a lie. Silence about a shipped retailer is a
> documentation gap; this rule is about self-contradiction only, and a tree cannot contradict a
> claim nobody made.*

That reasoning was sound when written and **this plan reverses it**, which is a different act from
fixing an oversight and has to be argued rather than assumed:

1. **Its premise is now false.** The objection was that demanding a section *"would redden the
   shipped tree"*. Once tasks 1 and 2 have written the two sections, demanding them reddens
   nothing. The cost the allowance was protecting against stops existing the moment the gap is
   filled.
2. **Criterion 1 overtakes it.** *"Each of Amazon, Target and Walmart has a recorded verdict in
   `docs/retailer-evidence.md`."* A requirement that the gap be filled outranks a rule that the gap
   need not be.
3. **"Not a lie" was the right test for rule 5 and is the wrong test for this.** Rule 5 is about
   self-contradiction. This is about the silent gap the module's own preamble opens with — and
   `README.md` does make a claim about both retailers, ✅ **Working**, with nothing in the evidence
   log behind it. The tree is not silent about them; only the evidence log is.

**So rule 7 is a reversal with a reason, not a repair.** Both the original allowance and this
reversal go into the script, beside each other.

## The rung question, decided here rather than drifted into

**Criterion 4 says a retailer whose honest position is rung 4 is recorded as rung 4, and that the
count of working retailers is not padded.** The temptation runs both ways and both are refused:

- **Padding up** — leaving Target and Walmart at ✅ Working because they *did* work in August.
  Three days of measurement says they have produced no resolved reading at all.
- **Padding down** — calling them rung 4 because they are refusing. **Rung 4 in this project means
  DROPPED, with evidence** — Pokémon Center is the precedent, and it was dropped on a *terms*
  finding, a permanent property of the retailer. Target and Walmart are refusing a client. That is
  a reputational state, not a policy one, and it can change without anybody doing anything.

**The honest third answer is that neither existing word fits, and inventing one is worse than
saying so.** The plan is to record the state precisely — *configured, refused at every rung tried,
no resolved reading since a stated date* — and to make the README say exactly that instead of
✅ Working. **Whether to drop them is Dan's call and `REQUIREMENTS.md` § *What is deliberately NOT
in this milestone* already says so**: REQ-25 produces the evidence, the decision is out of scope.

## What this plan must NOT do

- Not drop a retailer from `config/products.yaml`. That is the decision REQ-25 explicitly leaves to
  Dan, and doing it as a side effect of a matrix edit would be taking it for him.
- Not weaken rule 7 to make it pass. If it goes red on something real, that is the finding.
- Not touch `state.json`, `pacer-state.json` or `served/boty/status.json`.
