<objective>
Settle Amazon, cheaply, and build the gate that stops the phase's retailer count
from being padded later.

`.planning/ROADMAP.md` Phase 3 says to establish reachability cheaply *before*
investing in an adapter, and `03-CONTEXT.md` sharpens it: **check the Terms of
Use before the wall.** Pokémon Center's decisive finding in Phase 2 was not
technical — its ToU prohibits automated interaction outright, which is a better
and more durable reason than "we could not get in", because a wall can fall and
a written prohibition does not. Amazon's Conditions of Use are notoriously
explicit on exactly this point. Read them first. If Amazon forbids this in
writing, that settles it: record rung 4 and stop, rather than spending the plan
on transport work that should not ship anyway.

A REFUSED verdict is a successful completion of this plan, not a failure of it.
The roadmap's own criterion is "Amazon reports stock, **or** the support matrix
records what was tried and why it failed."

Purpose: REQ-07, and phase criteria 2, 4 and 7 — plus the machinery criterion 5
needs, because Phase 2's count clause has since gone vacuous (see design).

Output: an Amazon section in `docs/retailer-evidence.md` ending in exactly one
bold verdict line; a README matrix row; and `scripts/evidence_check.py`, an
executable gate that makes a padded retailer count fail rather than pass.
</objective>

<design>
**The evidence standard to match.** The Pokémon Center section of
`docs/retailer-evidence.md` is the bar: what was probed, what came back (HTTP
status, byte count, URL form, transport, cookie names), the quoted ToU clause, a
robots.txt excerpt, a prior-art review, and an explicit "what was NOT done, and
why". Record the observation first and draw the conclusion separately.

**The verdict grammar is load-bearing.** A section ends in exactly one line that
is character-for-character one of `**Verdict: REACHABLE (rung 1)**`,
`**Verdict: REACHABLE (rung 2)**`, `**Verdict: REACHABLE (rung 3)**` or
`**Verdict: REFUSED**`. There is deliberately no rung-4 REACHABLE form — rung 4
*is* REFUSED. Later gates grep this mechanically.

**Why Phase 2's count clause is no longer sufficient — this is the point of task
2.** 02-04 gated its retailer count with a clause of the form "five or more
retailers, OR `rung 4` appears in QUESTIONS.md and `**Verdict: REFUSED**`
appears in docs/retailer-evidence.md". That clause is now **satisfied by
documents that predate this phase**: the evidence doc already carries a REFUSED
verdict for Pokémon Center and QUESTIONS.md already contains the words `rung 4`.
Reused verbatim in Phase 3 it would be a gate that can never fail — worse than
no gate, because it looks like one. Phase 3's version has to be per-retailer,
and it has to close the other padding door too: reaching five by adding a
retailer that is not in the roadmap's scope at all.

**That second door is not hypothetical.** A control-only Micro Center was probed
in Phase 2, found viable at rung 1, and **explicitly declined** because it does
not carry the GO Plus + and could never alert on it. `03-CONTEXT.md` records
that the decision stands. This gate is what makes it stick without anyone having
to remember it.

**Three enforcement layers already exist; this gate sits above them rather than
replacing them.** `scripts/control_check.py` computes `configured - verified`
before any request; `boty.monitor.assess_health` fails a retailer whose control
cannot be read; `test_no_retailer_is_configured_without_a_page_we_have_actually_read`
requires a captured fixture, which `boty.fixtures.capture` only writes after a
live fetch that was not blocked. So a *fake* padded retailer already cannot go
green. What none of them catch is a padded retailer that is entirely **real** —
reachable, with a real control and a real fixture — but that cannot carry the
product this project exists for. That is the door task 2 closes.

**Politeness, and why it is not optional here.** Amazon is one of the two most
defended retailers on the consumer web, and Dan's monitor is currently green and
notifying from this host's IP. A block costs him the working monitor. Hard
limits for this plan: at most **6** requests to amazon.com in total across every
task, spaced at least **20 s** apart, at most one retry of any refusal and only
after a 120 s backoff, and an immediate stop if refusals start spreading rather
than an escalation to something noisier. If the Conditions of Use forbid
automated interaction, the correct number of requests is **zero**, and that is
what the record should be able to say.

**Reading the terms is not a monitor request.** Use WebFetch (or a single
`curl`) for the Conditions of Use — a one-off human-shaped read of a public
policy document — rather than `boty.fetch.get`. That keeps bot-y's own transport
out of amazon.com entirely until the question of whether it may go there is
settled, and it is the difference between the record being able to say "bot-y
makes no requests to amazon.com" and it not being able to.

**Rung 2 and the fresh-clone test.** `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md`'s
non-functional requirement is explicit: a retailer's PRIMARY path must work for
someone who clones the repo and adds no credentials, and a credential requiring
manual approval, a paid domain or a commercial agreement is a footnote rather
than support. Amazon's Product Advertising API is gated behind an Associates
account with qualifying sales. Evaluate it against that rule and record the
finding either way — Best Buy's row is the precedent for how an optional-only
API gets written up.

**If Amazon lands anyway.** `boty/retailers.py` has `amazon` in `MARKETPLACES`
already and **no** `FIRST_PARTY["amazon"]` entry, so an Amazon watch today falls
through the WR-03 escape hatch and reads UNKNOWN with "no first-party seller
list is configured for 'amazon'". Registering it means adding the exact
`offers.seller.name` string the live page uses, lowercased, and leaving `amazon`
in `MARKETPLACES` — it is the archetypal buy-box marketplace and the
unattributed-offer fallback must stay disabled there. Do not touch `_pick`. Do
not alter the `no structured stock data found` return text or its twelve spaces
of indentation: `scripts/mutation_check.py` M2 anchors on that exact source
substring, and a HARNESS ERROR there takes `make verify` down for a reason that
looks nothing like its cause.

**Importing a script from a test.** `tests/test_control_check.py` does it with
`importlib.util.spec_from_file_location` because `scripts/` is not a package.
Copy that idiom rather than inventing a new one. `scripts` is inside mypy's
`files` list, so every def in the new script needs full annotations.
</design>
