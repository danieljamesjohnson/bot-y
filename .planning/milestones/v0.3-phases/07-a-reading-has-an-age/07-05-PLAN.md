<objective>
Make the three surfaces say the age out loud, and make an absent one say UNKNOWN.

Four plans have put the facts in place: a stamp on every `Result` (07-01), a stamp that survives the
restart (07-02), one readable number for each retailer's current cadence (07-03), and a row for every
configured watch that says whether anybody looked (07-04). **Nothing yet renders any of it.** This
plan writes the two consumers — `cli._age_tag` and the dashboard's `ageTag` — and proves the third
surface, `status.json`, already carries everything a consumer needs.

**Purpose.** Dan asked when Amazon and Walmart were last read and the system had no answer. After
07-01…07-04 the answer exists in a file. This plan is where he can see it without opening one. It is
also where criterion 2 stops being a datum and becomes a rendering: `read_at: null` means *nobody
dated this reading*, and the only honest way to draw that is a mark that says so — never a blank,
which reads as fine, and never a number, which is an age nobody recorded.

**Output.** Two renderers with the same four forms; one duplicate formatter avoided by extracting
`fmtAge`'s bands rather than copying them; one register defect closed that 07-04 measured and could
not reach; and **two mutations on two different surfaces**, each watched going red by hand before the
harness ever ran either.

## The five criteria this phase is judged on (verbatim from ROADMAP.md, unedited)

  1. Every `Result` records when it was read, and that time is published per watch in `status.json`
  2. A reading with no recorded time is shown as UNKNOWN age, never as current — watched going red
  3. A reading older than its retailer's current interval is presented as stale in `status.json`, `boty check` and the dashboard, and the staleness is derived from the retailer's own pacing rather than a fixed clock
  4. The age survives a service restart, so a restart cannot make a two-day-old reading look fresh
  5. `make verify-offline` exits 0, and every gate this phase adds has been watched going red

**This plan owns the RENDERING half of criterion 2 and two of criterion 3's three surfaces.** The
datum half of criterion 2 is 07-01's (`None`, never `now`) and 07-02's (a stamp-less document loads
unknown). Criterion 3's threshold is 07-03's and its rows are 07-04's. Criterion 5 binds here as the
red-watch on **M36** and **M37**.

## `status.json` IS CRITERION 3's FIRST NAMED SURFACE, AND THIS PLAN ADDS NO CODE TO IT

A reader will look at `files_modified`, see no `boty/status.py`, and conclude a third of criterion 3
was skipped. It was not, and the argument is `07-PLAN-OUTLINE.md` § *Finding 7*'s, which this
repository wrote down for itself at `pacing.py:196-199` before this phase existed:

> *"stamping at write time would refresh the record forever and the age-out would never fire once —
> a bound that cannot bind is worse than no bound, because it reads like one in the file."*

A `stale: true/false` computed inside `status.write` fails the mirror way. A row written fresh
carries `stale: false`, and it goes on carrying `stale: false` for exactly the interval during which
it becomes stale. **So the file publishes raw facts and every consumer subtracts against its own
`now`** — `status.py:119-133`'s own recorded rule, applied for the sixth time in this phase.

What this plan owes that surface is therefore not a key but a **proof that the keys already there are
enough**: Task 1's join test writes a payload and derives a staleness verdict for a row using only
`w.read_at`, `w.checked` and the `current_interval_seconds` joined from the `retailers` array by
`retailer`, and asserts no fourth fact was needed and no `stale` key exists. That is the difference
between a criterion met and a criterion asserted.

## THE ARITHMETIC IN THE EARLIER PLANNING DOCUMENTS IS WRONG, AND THIS PLAN USES THE MEASURED NUMBER

`07-PLAN-OUTLINE.md` § *Finding 9* and `07-PATTERNS.md` both say *"a retailer at seven refusals is
legitimately on a ~97-minute cadence"*. **That figure is `skipped_reason`'s time REMAINING, which is
a different quantity from the interval.** 07-03 measured the interval and recorded the correction;
re-measured here off `pacer-state.json` at 09:41 today, against `MAX_BACKOFF_SECONDS = 6 * 60 * 60`
and `BACKOFF_FACTOR = 2.0`:

| Retailer | Standing interval | Refusals now | **Current interval** |
|---|---|---|---|
| target | 300 s | 7 | `min(300·2⁷, 21600)` = **21 600 s** (the 6-hour cap) |
| walmart | 300 s | 7 | **21 600 s** (the cap) |
| amazon | 1800 s (override) | 0 *(was 2 at 09:41)* | **1 800 s** *(was 7 200 s)* |
| gamestop | 900 s (override) | 0 | **900 s** |
| bestbuy, nintendo | 300 s | 0 | **300 s** |

**This table moved while this plan was being written, and that is the sharpest argument in it.**
Amazon sat at 2 refusals — a 7 200 s cadence — at 09:41 and had reset to 0 by 09:52, because it
answered and `Pacer.save` omits a retailer at zero refusals. **A threshold that is correct for a
retailer at 09:41 is wrong for the same retailer eleven minutes later**, which is why criterion 3
says *derived from the retailer's own pacing* and why neither consumer may hold a constant. The two
rows M37's `breaks=` rests on — the 21 600 s cap and the 900 s override — did not move, and the
executor should **re-measure the table rather than quoting these numbers**: only the arithmetic
(`min(interval · 2^refusals, 21600)`) is stable.

**The planning documents are NOT edited.** This is recorded beside them, on Phase 3.1's precedent and
on 07-03's and 07-04's, both of which did exactly this. It matters concretely twice: the tag forms in
this plan's worked examples read `[age 7h > 6h]` rather than `[age 7h > 97m]`, and **M37's `breaks=`
sentence is built on this table** — see below, where it is the reason a single fixed threshold is
wrong in *both* directions at once.

## WHY THE 30-MINUTE CONSTANT ON THIS PAGE MUST NOT BE BORROWED

`served/boty/index.html:137` carries `const stale = age > 1800;`, driving the *"the monitor may not be
running"* banner. It is correct where it is and it is **not touched**. § *Finding 9* forbids merging

- **gamestop** is on a 900 s cadence. A 1800 s threshold calls a 25-minute-old GameStop reading

- **target and walmart** are on the 21 600 s cap. A 1800 s threshold paints every remembered row of

One constant, wrong in opposite directions on the same page in the same second. The two questions are
different — the banner asks *"is this snapshot being written?"*, the row asks *"is this reading
younger than the cadence its own retailer is currently on?"* — and the distinction goes in a comment
where the next reader will look, because the next person to see two staleness rules on one page will
try to merge them.

## THE FOUR FORMS, AND WHY BOTH SURFACES USE THE SAME FOUR

`_store_tag` enumerates four forms in its docstring and prints `?` rather than a blank *"because 'the
page did not tell us' is a fact worth printing"*. An age has the same shape — two values, either of
which can be absent — so it has the same four forms, and **the two surfaces answer with the same
four** so a reader comparing `boty check` to the page never has to translate:

| State | `boty check` | dashboard | weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| stamp present, within the retailer's cadence | `[age 4s]` | `4s ago` | label |
| stamp present, past its retailer's cadence | `[age 7h > 6h]` | `7h ago > 6h` | **warning** |
| stamp present, cadence unknown | `[age 3h, cadence ?]` | `3h ago · cadence ?` | **warning** |
| no stamp | `[age ?]` | `age ?` | **warning** |

**The stale form prints BOTH numbers, and that is a decision.** A bare `stale` cannot say what it was
judged against, and criterion 3's entire content is *which* threshold was used. `[age 7h > 6h]` is
the `[store Y != pinned X]` idiom one field over: show the two operands and let the reader see the
comparison rather than trust the verdict.

**`>` and not `>=`.** A reading exactly one cadence old is due to be replaced this instant, not
overdue. Asserted to the float at the boundary in Task 1 rather than left to a reader's assumption.

**And `_age_tag` NEVER returns `None`, which is where it departs from `_store_tag`.** `_store_tag`
returns `None` because five of six retailers can never produce a store and their rows must stay
clean. Every reading, by contrast, either has an age or has a stated absence of one — there is no
clean-row case. If fresh rows carried no tag, an absent tag would *mean* fresh, and an implicit claim
is precisely what this phase removes. The tag is unconditional and its return type says so.

## THE `storeTag` REGISTER DEFECT, WHICH 07-04 MEASURED AND HANDED HERE

07-04 § *Measurement note 3* records it and could not fix it, because `served/boty/index.html` is
this plan's file. Restated with its argument, because this plan is asked either to fix it or to argue
it, and it fixes it:

`storeTag` renders `store: null` with a non-null `store_pinned` as a **warn** tag reading
`store ? · pinned X`, titled *"this watch is pinned to a store, but the page did not say which store
answered — the reading may be about somewhere else"*. On a **remembered** row 07-04 publishes
`store: null` always — the ledger holds no store — so that is the only branch such a row can reach.
The sentence is true in the letter (no page said anything) and **false in register**: it describes a
reading that happened and failed to name its store, on a row whose actual story is *nobody looked
this cycle*.

It cannot fire on this host today, because `WALMART_STORE_ID` is unset and `store_pinned` is `null`
too. **It fires the day Dan pins a store** — which is `QUESTIONS.md` § 0f, the open question 07-06's
checkpoint puts in front of him. Shipping a defect that appears the moment he answers a question this
phase asks him is not a latent bug; it is a scheduled one.

**The fix is an early return, and it is small because the argument is sharp.** `storeTag`'s own
comment says it exists to distinguish *"no store recorded"* from *"store B answered and you pinned
A"* — **both of which are statements about a reading.** A row with no reading is in neither state, so
rendering it in either is the conflation this phase exists to remove. `if (w.checked === false)
return '';`, and `=== false` rather than `!w.checked` because an old `status.json` sitting on disk
during a deploy has no `checked` key at all, and `!undefined` is `true`, which would silently
suppress the store tag on every row of that file. That is 07-02's shape-tolerance rule applied one
file over.
